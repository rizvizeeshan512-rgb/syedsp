Chapter two · The people

A culture of
generous welcome.

The most memorable parts of a journey are often the small moments shared along the way.

A living tradition

Warmth is a
way of life.

Omani culture is shaped by family, community, faith, and a long history of exchange with the world. Hospitality is an everyday practice, expressed in the time people make for one another.

Visitors may be welcomed with kahwa—Omani coffee often flavoured with cardamom—and dates. Traditional music, poetry, and dance mark celebrations and gatherings. Craft traditions such as silverwork, weaving, pottery, and khanjar making continue to carry local identity across generations.

A few ways to connect

Notice the details.

Approach customs with respect and curiosity. A little awareness makes every encounter more comfortable.

01

The coffee welcome

Kahwa and dates are commonly offered to guests. Accepting a small serving is a gracious way to join the moment; follow your host’s lead.

02

Dress with care

Modest clothing is appreciated, particularly in villages and religious settings. At mosques, check visitor guidance and cover shoulders and legs.

03

Ask before a photo

Always seek permission before photographing people. Respect signs and local guidance around religious sites and private spaces.

04

Greet and take your time

A warm “as-salaam alaykum” is a thoughtful greeting. Give conversations time; patience and courtesy are valued.
