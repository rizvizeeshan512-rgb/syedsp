Chapter two · The people

A culture of
generous welcome.

Hospitality is made of small gestures: coffee poured for a guest, a greeting returned warmly, and time set aside for conversation.

A living tradition

Warmth is a
way of life.

Omani culture is shaped by family, community, faith, and a long history of exchange with the world. Customs vary from place to place and household to household, so the best guide is always your host.

A traditional welcome may include kahwa, Omani coffee flavoured with cardamom and poured into a small finjan, alongside dates. Hosts may refill a guest’s cup; a gentle shake of the cup signals that you have had enough. Sharing food and sitting together are valued parts of making a visitor feel at ease.

Music, poetry, and dance accompany celebrations and community gatherings. In the souq, silverwork, textiles, pottery, and incense reflect skills and materials tied to local life. The khanjar—the curved ceremonial dagger—is a recognised emblem of Omani identity and is worn on formal occasions.

A few ways to connect

Notice the details.

Be attentive, follow local guidance, and ask when you are unsure. Respectful curiosity makes travel more rewarding.

01

The coffee welcome

Take a small cup of kahwa and a date if offered. Coffee is usually poured in small servings; a polite cup shake can indicate you are finished.

02

Dress with care

Choose modest clothing in villages and public settings. Mosques have their own visitor rules, so check current hours and dress requirements before going.

03

Ask before a photo

Ask permission before photographing people. Follow posted restrictions at religious sites and avoid photographing private spaces without consent.

04

Make room for a greeting

“As-salaam alaykum” is a common greeting. A friendly response and a little patience help conversations start naturally.

05

Taste the local table

Look for shuwa at festive gatherings, rice dishes such as majboos, and Omani halwa. Recipes and serving traditions vary by family and region.
