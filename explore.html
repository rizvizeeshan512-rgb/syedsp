Chapter three · The journey

One country.
Many worlds.

Build a route that moves from Muscat’s coast into the interior, then leave space for the mountains, wadis, and southern monsoon landscapes.

Where to begin

Six places to feel.

These highlights span city, heritage, mountain, desert, wadi, and coast. Driving distances are long, so give each region time.

01

Capital · Coast

Muscat

Visit the Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque, then head to Mutrah for the Corniche, fish market, and covered souq. Old Muscat’s forts and waterfront palaces offer a different view of the capital’s history.

Architecture · Markets · Sea
02

Interior · Heritage

Nizwa

Nizwa Fort’s massive circular tower looks over the old town and its souq. Use the city as a base for nearby villages, date-palm oases, and other forts in the Dakhiliyah region.

Fort · Souq · Oasis towns
03

The Hajar Mountains

Jebel Akhdar

On the Saiq Plateau, mountain villages and terraces hold orchards of pomegranates, apricots, and roses. Take a marked village walk and look for the falaj channels that carry water through the gardens.

Hiking · Orchards · Highlands
04

Eastern sands

Wahiba Sands

Also known as Sharqiyah Sands, this sea of dunes changes colour with the light. Go with an experienced local guide or a suitable high-clearance 4WD, and plan an overnight stay for sunset and stargazing.

Desert · Stargazing · Local guides
05

Al Sharqiyah South · Wadi

Wadi Shab

A short boat crossing leads to a trail through limestone cliffs and palm groves, followed by clear natural pools. Reaching the inner cave and waterfall involves swimming through a narrow passage; bring water, secure footwear, and swim only when conditions are suitable.

Hiking · Swimming · Canyon
06

Southern Oman

Dhofar

During khareef, mist and seasonal drizzle turn the hills around Salalah green. Outside the monsoon, explore the coast and the Land of Frankincense sites, including Al Baleed and Khor Rori (Sumhuram).

Khareef · Coast · Frankincense

Make the most of the road

Travel well.

Conditions vary by season and terrain. Check current local guidance before setting out.

01 / WHEN

Choose your season

Oman’s cooler, drier winter generally runs from November to early March. Dhofar’s khareef usually lasts from mid-June to September; expect mist, light rain, and changing visibility.

02 / HOW

Plan for the route

Some mountain roads and desert routes require a high-clearance 4WD. Confirm rental terms and access rules, check weather and road conditions, and avoid remote routes without local advice.

03 / WHAT

Pack for the day

Carry more water than you expect to need, sun protection, and shoes suited to uneven ground. For wadis, check water conditions and never enter a flooded channel.

04 / RESPECT

Be a considerate guest

Dress modestly in public and follow each mosque’s visitor rules. Ask before photographing people, and leave historic and natural sites as you found them.
