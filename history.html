Chapter one · The story

History written
across the land.

In Oman, the past is visible in the forts above old towns, the channels that still move water, and the ports that once joined Arabia to the Indian Ocean.

A long view

Rooted here.
Connected everywhere.

Oman’s geography shaped its history. The Hajar Mountains, interior oases, and long coast created very different ways of living—and routes that brought communities into contact with neighbours far beyond the peninsula.

Archaeological evidence points to ancient copper production and exchange in the interior. On the coast, ports connected Oman to East Africa, India, and the Gulf. In Dhofar, frankincense resin travelled from tree groves through inland waystations and coastal harbours. These were not isolated episodes; they were networks built and maintained over generations.

Water was equally important. Aflaj—channels that carry water from springs, underground sources, or seasonal flows—made agriculture and settlement possible in dry landscapes. Their upkeep depended on shared rights and community cooperation.

Explore these stories in the landscape: start with Bahla Fort, walk around an oasis, or visit the archaeological sites of the Land of Frankincense in Dhofar.

A few moments in time

Oman through the ages.

These milestones connect the country’s engineering, trade, and architecture with places you can still visit.

Around 2500 BCE

Water is carefully shared

Archaeological evidence for irrigation in Oman reaches back thousands of years. The aflaj systems channel water to homes and fields; about 3,000 remain in use.

12th–15th centuries

Bahla grows in influence

The Banu Nebhan made Bahla their capital. Its vast mud-brick fort, oasis, Friday Mosque, and defensive wall reveal the scale of the settlement.

Across the Indian Ocean

Ports connect regions

Omani mariners and merchants linked Arabian ports with East Africa, India, and the Gulf, carrying goods, skills, and ideas between coastal communities.

World heritage today

Places protected and shared

Bahla Fort, the Aflaj Irrigation Systems, and the Land of Frankincense are among Oman’s UNESCO-listed heritage places.
