SULTANATE OF OMAN · ARABIAN PENINSULA

A place of contrasts

Where old routes
meet new horizons.

Oman stretches from the Strait of Hormuz to the Arabian Sea. Mountains, gravel plains, date-palm oases, long beaches, and desert all sit within one remarkable landscape.

Its maritime history connected the coast to East Africa, India, and the Gulf. Inland, communities developed sophisticated ways to share scarce water and grow food in a dry climate. Those stories still shape the places you can visit today.

The story behind the place

Heritage lives
in the walls.

Omani heritage is not confined to museums. It can be read in Bahla’s mud-brick ramparts, the water channels that still feed farms, and the frankincense landscapes of Dhofar.

History in the landscape

Stories carried by place.

Three threads help explain how people in Oman traded, farmed, and built communities across different environments.

01 / WATER

The falaj way

For centuries, aflaj channels have guided water from springs and underground sources to homes and fields. The systems depend on shared rules for dividing and maintaining water.

02 / TRADE

Frankincense country

Frankincense harvested in Dhofar travelled through ports and caravan routes. The Land of Frankincense World Heritage site links groves, ports, and inland waystations.

03 / WELCOME

Culture, day to day

Kahwa and dates, a visit to the souq, and time spent talking are ordinary ways hospitality becomes part of a visitor’s experience.
